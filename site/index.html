Search a billion vectors from a laptop. p99 under 12 ms.

sieve is a vector search engine written from scratch in Rust. It rebuilds the design Exa described in How we built a web-scale vector database, then measures it: 1 billion vectors and 100 GB of raw data, served from one 24 GB laptop with no servers and no GPU.

0.0 ms
  1. RoutePick the nearest of 131,072 cluster centres.
  2. ScanScore their vectors with 1-bit codes held in RAM.
  3. RerankRead the best candidates exactly from SSD, in parallel.
  4. ReturnThe ten nearest. 2.7 ms median.

Head-to-head from SSD

Against Microsoft’s DiskANN, level on throughput and 7–10× faster per query.

DiskANN is the standard index for data that doesn’t fit in memory. Both ran on the same laptop over the first 10M SPACEV vectors, with the same queries and ground truth. Caches were cleared before each run and each used about 0.5 GB of RAM. DiskANN leads at low recall. Above 0.94 its chain of dependent reads gets long, while sieve sends one parallel batch.

Where it loses

With everything in RAM, a graph wins by 45–50×.

In memory, DiskANN’s graph walk scores about 1,840 vectors per query. sieve’s cluster scan scores about 222,000 compressed codes. Each code is about 9× cheaper, but there are 120× more of them. Exa’s design doesn’t target this case. The known fix is many smaller clusters routed by a graph, as in Microsoft’s SPANN.

How it was measured

One laptop, public data, and every run reproducible.

Exa’s post described the design but published no recall or latency numbers. Every figure here comes from logs committed to the repository.

  • One machineApple M4 Pro, 12 cores, 24 GB of RAM, internal SSD. No servers, no GPU, no cloud.
  • Public benchmarksMS SPACEV-1B (100-d int8) with official ground truth, and 113.5M MS MARCO v2.1 passages.
  • Cold means coldIndex files are evicted from the OS page cache before every SSD run, checked with mincore.
  • Fair to DiskANNIts disk index had no macOS read path. A small patch adds uncached pread, matching its Linux path.
  • One recall metricThe head-to-head scores both systems with DiskANN’s strict recall@10. The 1B figures are tie-aware, within 0.001.
  • Also at 113.5M passagesRecall 0.909 at 4.8 ms median with codes in RAM, or 0.902 at 13 ms in 225 MB.
